Vending for RV resorts and marinas in Tampa Bay: what guests actually buy

Theo Larkins, Founder Oct 2026

RV resorts and marinas have something in common: guests are often far from the nearest store, they spend a lot of time outdoors, and they tend to need small things at inconvenient times. Forgotten sunscreen, a cold drink after launching the boat, or a snack for the kids after the pool closes. Well-planned vending fills that gap and makes the property feel more complete.

Here is a practical look at what tends to work at these properties in the Tampa Bay area.

What guests reach for

Every property is a little different, and the best product mix comes from watching what sells and adjusting. That said, a few categories make sense almost everywhere outdoors in Florida:

  • Cold drinks, and plenty of them. Water, sports drinks, sparkling water, sodas, and iced teas. In Florida heat, hydration is the core of the menu.
  • Grab-and-go snacks. Chips, nuts, trail mix, jerky, granola and protein bars. Items that travel well on a boat or a hike.
  • Treats for families. Candy and familiar favorites, especially near pools, playgrounds, and activity areas.
  • Breakfast and quick bites. Pastries, breakfast bars, and similar items for early departures and anglers heading out before sunrise.
  • EYN stocks everyday essentials like sunscreen and pain relievers, and our retail-style machines can carry larger convenience items.

Seasonal shifts matter too. Snowbird season, summer family travel, holiday weekends, and fishing tournaments can all change what sells. A good operator adjusts the mix as the calendar changes.

Placement that matches guest flow

Where the machine goes is as important as what is in it. Good locations at RV resorts and marinas tend to be places guests already pass through:

  • The camp store or office entrance, for after-hours coverage
  • Bathhouses and laundry buildings
  • The pool, clubhouse, or activity center
  • Near the boat ramp, fuel dock, or dockmaster's office
  • Ship's store or lounge areas at marinas

The goal is to put products where the need happens, not where there happens to be an empty wall.

Equipment that stands up to the setting

Salt air, humidity, sun, and summer storms are hard on equipment. Machines are chosen to look polished in a resort setting, not just to survive salt air. We place equipment in covered or indoor spots and service it regularly. Those placements can include a smart cooler, which we offer today alongside traditional machines.

For larger sites, we can discuss a micro market buildout.

Cashless payment is a must. Many guests rarely carry cash, especially on the water, and tap-to-pay with cards, phones, and watches keeps sales simple.

Complementing your camp store or ship's store

If your property already has a store, vending does not need to compete with it. It can cover the hours when the store is closed, or sit in parts of the property that are a long walk from the store. Many properties find that this kind of after-hours coverage is what guests appreciate most.

Hands-off for your staff

Front desk and dock staff already have plenty to do. With a full-service operator, your team does not handle stocking, cash, or repairs. EYN Vending installs the equipment, keeps it stocked and clean, and services it, so your team can focus on guests.
